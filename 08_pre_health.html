Overview

Your advising dean helps pre-health students:

  • Explore the range of health careers (medicine, dentistry, veterinary medicine, nursing, PA, pharmacy, and more)
  • Design an academic plan that fits your interests and professional goals
  • Plan extracurricular activities for health professional school preparation
  • Manage academic challenges and mental health impacts
  • Identify research, internship, and experiential learning opportunities
  • Practice interviews and get general application advice

A&S's liberal arts model is excellent preparation for health careers — broad critical thinking, writing, and analytical skills complement the required science coursework.

Academic Preparation

Choosing a Major

Cornell has no pre-med major. Choose any of the 41 A&S majors based on genuine interest. What matters is your GPA, prerequisite coursework, and preparation — not the major itself. Declare by end of sophomore year.

Prerequisite Coursework

Required and recommended courses vary by health profession. The HPAC's Pre-Health Academic Preparation page details requirements for medicine, dentistry, vet medicine, and others. All required coursework must be taken for a letter grade (A–F) — no S/U.

Using AP / IB Credits

The decision to use AP/IB credits is yours. Acceptance varies by professional school — check the requirements of each school you may apply to. If you use AP credit, consider taking an upper-level course to demonstrate proficiency in that subject area.

Activities Planning

Professional schools don't rank activity types — they want to know why these experiences matter to you. Plan thoughtfully, not as a checklist. Balance activities with academics.

Clinical Experiences
Shadow a physician/dentist through personal networks, Handshake, cold outreach, or volunteering in local clinical settings. Consider EMT certification or working as a CNA or medical scribe.
Service Experiences
Teaching assistant or tutor positions (LSC, Knight Institute, OUB), Cornell student employment, Einhorn Center volunteer opportunities. Paid employment counts.
Research & Scholarly Activities
A&S undergraduate research programs, Nexus Scholars Program (summer), Humanities Scholars Program, independent study, or directly emailing faculty whose research interests you.
Leadership Experiences
Intergroup Dialogue Program (IDP) for cultural competency, student organization leadership (ClubFest is a good starting point).
When to Engage
  • Research is available year-round — determine what schedule fits your coursework
  • Summer and winter breaks are ideal for time-intensive activities like clinical shadowing or structured programs
  • Structured summer programs typically require applications from January–March — plan ahead

Dean's Certification Letter

Some professional schools require a Dean's Certification Letter (also called College Questionnaire or College Certification).

1
Complete the Information Release Request form
Authorizes Cornell to release your academic and disciplinary record. Allow four weeks for processing. The letter is then prepared and kept on file.
2
Submit the school's Dean's Certification form
Include a stamped envelope addressed to the school. Submit at least four weeks before the school's application deadline.
External transfer students: provide both your Cornell transcript and your transcript from your previous institution when applying to professional schools.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I take an honors/upper-level course if I'm using AP credit?
Personal choice. If you're confident in your AP preparation, consider it — but it's not required by professional schools. It can demonstrate proficiency in that subject area.
Do I need to take biology, chemistry, and math in my first semester?
Not necessarily. Your plan should reflect your comfort zone. You have four years to complete prerequisites — don't rush. Talk to your advising dean to build a schedule that fits your needs.
Can I study abroad if I'm pre-health?
Yes. Work with a study abroad advisor to plan around your prerequisite timeline. Sample academic plans can help guide that conversation.
Should I take a bridge year?
Meet with the Health Professions Advising Center (HPAC) to discuss your specific situation and timeline.
