Choosing a Major / Minor

A&S offers 41 majors. Take time to explore before committing — the liberal arts model is designed for it.

Not sure where to start? Schedule time with your advising dean or career counselor.

Overview & Timeline

Year 1–2
Explore courses across departments. All A&S students start as undeclared — this is intentional.
Sophomore Year
Most students declare a major during sophomore year. You must declare prior to the start of your junior year to maintain good academic standing.
After Soph.
If you cannot enter a major by end of sophomore year, meet with your advising dean immediately and complete the Late Declaration of Major form.

Applying for a Major

You apply (declare) as soon as you've completed the prerequisites — sometimes as early as end of first year.

1
Check prerequisites
Each department sets its own requirements. Review the department website and University Catalog for specifics.
2
Submit an application
Some majors accept online applications; others require you to meet with the Director of Undergraduate Studies (DUS) and complete the application with them.
3
Get confirmed
Once accepted, your major is declared and a faculty advisor is assigned. Your DUST record updates accordingly.
If you cannot declare by end of sophomore year, contact your advising dean to review options and complete the Late Declaration of Major form via the Registrar.
Department Websites Directors of Undergraduate Studies Late Declaration Form

Second or Third Major

  • Additional majors must be within the College of Arts & Sciences
  • Can be added in junior or even senior year — apply as early as possible to allow time to complete requirements
  • Follow the same application process as the first major
  • Consider whether a minor or elective coursework might satisfy your interests without the full commitment of a second major
Consult with both your faculty advisor and advising dean before pursuing a second or third major.

Adding a Minor

A minor is a secondary area of study requiring 15–18 credits. It's optional, but useful for grouping related electives meaningfully. Many minors in other Cornell colleges are open to A&S students.

  • Browse the full list of available minors at cornell.edu/academics/fields.cfm
  • Contact the office for your desired minor to learn their process for adding it
  • Discuss course double-counting (minor + major) with both departments before assuming it's allowed
Browse All Cornell Minors

Dropping a Major or Minor

To drop a major or minor, contact the undergraduate coordinator or the Director of Undergraduate Studies (DUS) in the relevant department. No petition is typically required — the DUS handles the change.

Pre-Health & Pre-Law

There is no required or recommended major for students pursuing health or legal careers. Choose a major based on your genuine intellectual interests.

Pre-Health Students
Consult Pre-Health Resources for course planning, activities, and dean's certification letters. The Health Professions Advising Center (HPAC) is your central hub.
Pre-Law Students
Consult A&S Career Development for law school advising, resources, and support.
